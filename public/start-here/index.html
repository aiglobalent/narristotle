New to Narristotle? Walk the method in order. Every step below points at something already published — no new lessons were written for this page.

STEP 1

What is story structure?

The first principle: from idea to a path forward. A story is not a pile of ideas; it is a path a reader can follow.

The Method: Story Structure →

STEP 2

Character arcs: give change a reason.

The second principle: a character who changes, and a reason for the change. Study it in a classic — Elizabeth Bennet changes through misjudgment and discovery.

The Method: Character Transformation →  Pride and Prejudice study notes →

STEP 3

Setups & payoffs: plant early, pay off later.

The third principle: give an early detail a reason to matter later. The demonstration — she keeps the key instead of selling it, and when the door locks behind her, that choice becomes her way out.

The Method: Setups & Payoffs →  A Twist Is Only as Good as the Promise You Planted →

STEP 4

Cause & effect: choices create momentum.

The fourth principle: every choice has a consequence, and consequences are what pull a reader forward. Start with one decision, then follow the trouble it creates.

The Method: Dramatic Cause & Effect →  Why “Waiting for the Muse” Is Just Procrastination With Better PR →
