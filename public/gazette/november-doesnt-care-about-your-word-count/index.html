November Doesn't Care About Your Word Count

November showed up without its old host, and the replacements are already arguing about rules. Forget the leaderboard: a month of typing is not a month of writing.

THE SHORT ANSWER

NaNoWriMo is gone, and its replacements are already arguing about rules, word counts, and machine help. The month still works if you use it for one thing: a scene where somebody wants something and pays for it.

November showed up without its old host. The nonprofit behind NaNoWriMo closed last year, and this month's replacements range from a 50,000-word sprint to a screenwriters' 90-page dash. The rules differ, especially on machine help. Some ban it outright. Others say nothing at all.

Forget the leaderboard for a minute. A month of typing is not a month of writing. Writers hit a daily target and still dodge the one scene that scares them. The target becomes the hiding place.

Try a smaller bet instead. One scene, where somebody wants something specific and the price is clear. Picture a writer on a Kauai lanai, notebook open, with one note: let the villain win the argument. Write until that argument lands.

That is what this week's conferences are really selling, underneath the panels and pitch sessions. Kauai's master classes run through Thursday, and its conference opens Friday. Go for the note you can use on Monday, not the tote bag.

So pick your challenge, pick your rule, and then ignore both when the scene gets good. Thirty days from now, nobody will ask how many words you typed on November 3. They will ask what happened in the scene. Make sure something did.

Bring the idea. Even the messy version.
