November Is Coming. Your Outline Is Not a Shield.

November starts tomorrow. The plan is not the draft, and the draft is allowed to be dreadful. That is the whole dare.

THE SHORT ANSWER

November starts tomorrow, and every writer with a plan is about to learn the same lesson: the plan is not the draft. The only November rule that matters is to write the messy pages now and let December do the judging.

Tomorrow the calendar flips, and a thousand beautiful plans will meet their first ugly paragraph. That is not a tragedy. That is the entire point of November.

The old challenge is gone in name only: the nonprofit behind it closed last year, but the dare survived, because a dare does not need a headquarters. Fifty thousand words, thirty days, one draft that is allowed to be dreadful.

Here is the contrarian part. Do not spend November polishing the first chapter until it gleams while chapter two sits unwritten. A gleaming first chapter and no second chapter is not a novel; it is a very tidy cul-de-sac.

Use the Method as a labeled demonstration, not a leash: one want, one obstacle, one change on the last line, then the next scene. Cause and effect will carry you further than adjectives ever will.

Try it tonight in the Arcade, if you like, with a writer, a haunted attic, and a promise made at midnight. Then close the Arcade, open the draft, and keep the promise yourself. November does not care how clever your outline was. It only counts the pages.

Bring the idea. Even the messy version.

Your notes, your voice, your story. Let’s get to the draft.
