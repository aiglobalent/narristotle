THE SHORT ANSWER

Your pitch is not a summary; it is a trailer. Conference week is here — Jackson Hole opens today, Bouchercon tomorrow — and the writers who get remembered are the ones whose collision an agent cannot unsee.

Every October, thousands of writers check into conference hotels carrying the same contraband: a novel in a tote bag and a pitch they have rehearsed in the shower. They will practice it in the elevator. They will deliver it to the waffle iron. And when the moment comes — an actual agent, an actual hallway — half of them will describe their book as "a story about a guy who learns some stuff."

Here is the gadfly's unpopular opinion: your pitch is not a summary. It is a trailer. Nobody ever bought a ticket because a trailer listed the plot in order. They bought it because one image, one collision, one impossible promise made them need the next two hours.

The hallway pitch has three moving parts. A person the listener can see. A collision they cannot unsee. And a comp title that does the shelf-placement for you — Succession meets Paw Patrol in space is a joke until you realize an agent immediately knows exactly where it lives and who it is for. That is not selling out. That is giving a busy stranger a handhold.

This week the hallways are real: the Jackson Hole Writers Conference opens today with agents and editors taking meetings, and Bouchercon starts tomorrow in Calgary. If you are in one of those hallways, rehearse out loud — to a friend, to the ice machine, to anyone. A pitch you have only ever read silently is a draft, and you know what we say about drafts.

And if you want a sparring partner before the real ambush, the Arcade has a new toy this week: Conference Pitch Roulette deals you an exhausted writer, a bizarre dropped object, and one unhinged comp title, and dares you to pitch through it. Absurd reps build real muscles. Writing is rewriting — and pitching, it turns out, is pitching again.

The takeaway: Absurd reps build real muscles. Writing is rewriting — and pitching, it turns out, is pitching again.
