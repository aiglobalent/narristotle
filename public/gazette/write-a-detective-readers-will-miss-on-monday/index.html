Write a Detective Readers Will Miss on Monday

THE SHORT ANSWER

Bouchercon's panel on sustaining a detective character points at the real series engine: not cleverer murders, but a detective with private unfinished business. A series survives on the person, not the puzzle.

Bouchercon closes today in Calgary, and the panel worth stealing from its schedule ran yesterday: Sustaining a Detective Character, with Guest of Honor Kathy Reichs among the panelists. The question behind that title is the whole series game. Why does a reader come back for book nine when they already know how the detective thinks?

The Gadfly's contrarian answer is that it is not the murders. Murders are interchangeable, and a clever one buys you exactly one book. What sustains a detective is private unfinished business: the grudge, the debt, the phone call they keep not making. Give the detective a life that gets worse in interesting ways between cases, and readers will follow the person, not the puzzle.

Test it with the Method's favorite check, labeled demonstration: each book should change the detective, therefore the next case lands differently, but the old wound still pulls. If your detective could be swapped for another trench coat and nothing in the story changed, you do not have a series yet. You have a plot wearing a name tag.

The practical move is to write the scene where your detective is not solving anything. Making coffee badly. Dodging a phone call. Losing an argument with someone they love. If that scene bores you, the series has no engine. If it is the scene readers would quote back to you, the murders will take care of themselves.

If you want low-stakes reps before you commit to book one, the Arcade is built for exactly this kind of play: deal a detective, a secret they will never tell anyone, and a town they swore they would never revisit, then write the scene where they are not solving anything. A detective readers will miss on Monday is not written in the interrogation room. They are written in the kitchen, avoiding the call.
